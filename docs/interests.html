What I like working on

Research Interests

I like questions where brain signals, movement, and hardware meet — and where the end result is something that can actually be used in the real world.

Neural signals for memory and navigation

How the brain keeps track of where we are and where we want to go.

I’m especially interested in theta-band activity in the hippocampus and related areas, and how these rhythms change when we plan, recall, or move through space — in both animals and humans.

  • Planning vs movement-related changes in theta power.
  • How goals and context show up in LFP/EEG features.
  • Connecting virtual tasks with more natural behaviour.

Wearable gait sensing with IMUs

Making foot-mounted IMUs useful outside the lab.

I like building insole-based systems that estimate trajectories and gait parameters from a few small sensors, then seeing how close we can get to motion capture on real tracks and pavements.

  • Trajectory estimation with ZUPT and Kalman filters.
  • Handling drift and magnetometer issues in the wild.
  • Using gait as a window into ageing and disease.

Brain–machine interfaces & feedback

Closing the loop between neural activity and useful actions.

I’m drawn to BMIs where the user gets clear feedback — visual, haptic, or movement — and the system adapts based on how the brain responds, not just one-way decoding.

  • EEG/LFP-based decoding of intent and state.
  • Shared control between user and device.
  • Tasks that separate prediction, action and feedback.

Signal processing & clean pipelines

Making messy data slightly less messy (and easier to reuse).

A lot of my time goes into building simple, transparent processing chains for EEG, LFP and IMU data, and making sure the stats and plots actually mean what we think they mean.

  • Time–frequency analysis and spectral modelling (e.g. FOOOF).
  • Artifact handling and honest quality checks.
  • Reproducible code that other people can actually run.
