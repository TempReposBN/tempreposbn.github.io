What I like working on

Research Interests

I like questions where brain signals, movement, and hardware meet — and where the end result is something that can actually be used in the real world.

Neural signals

Neuroscience appeals to me because it sits at the intersection of biology, computation, and engineering—where you can both explain brain function and build systems that interface with it.

Wearable gait sensing with IMUs

Making foot-mounted IMUs useful outside the lab.

I like building and optimising existing systems that are proposed, then seeing how I could think about ways to improve them, even if its not successful in creating groundbreaking work or revolutionising IMU industry, its give me satisfaction from trying to work out the problem.

  • Trajectory estimation with ZUPT and Kalman filters.
  • Handling drift and magnetometer issues in the wild.
  • Using gait as a window into ageing and disease.

Signal processing

Making messy data slightly less messy.

A lot of my time goes into building simple, processing chains for EEG, LFP and IMU data, and making sure the stats and plots actually mean what we think they mean and do what we want them to do.

  • LFP data processing, IMU data processing and EEG data processing.
  • Artifact handling and honest quality checks.
  • Reproducible code that other people can actually run.
