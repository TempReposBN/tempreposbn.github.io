About Me

I’m Eric, a Biomedical Engineering graduate from NUS who likes problems where brain signals, movement, and hardware have to work together — ideally in ways that end up helping real people.

A brief version

I’m Eric, I recently finished my B.Eng. in Biomedical Engineering at the National University of Singapore, graduating with Highest Distinction. Along the way I helped win the Medical Grand Challenge and spent a lot of time in labs working with brain and motion data.

On the research side, I’ve worked on hippocampal theta during goal-directed virtual navigation in macaques (N.1 Lab), foot-mounted IMU pipelines for real-world gait on tracks and pavements (NUS iHealthtech), and EEG experiments on active vs. passive touch in VR (EPFL TNE Lab). Most of my work are within the intersection of signal processing, neuroscience, and embedded systems.

Outside of formal projects, I like to build things! From nixie-tube clocks to 3D-printed fixtures to Apps. Lately I’ve been playing with an EMOTIV EPOC X EEG headset and sketching ideas for small, personal model to replace a few everyday actions.

Outside of research, I’m usually lifting at the gym, shooting archery, hiking, or travelling. Being outdoors and moving around keeps me sane and motivated.

Journey in NUS

At NUS (2021–2025) I grew from a kid with a wild brain–computer dream into a researcher. I graduated with Highest Distinction in Biomedical Engineering, helped win the Medical Grand Challenge, led the Archery Club, and contributed to projects on brain signals and gait in N.1 Lab and iHealthtech.

My dream

Since I was young, I’ve wanted to connect brain and computer so I could “live inside ideas” instead of just reading about them. I imagined plugging my mind in and walking around worlds I built myself. Years later, that’s still the direction I’m moving towards.

DIY nixie clock

I built a nixie clock using old tubes from the USSR, wrapped in a cleaner, modern case. It doesn’t do much beyond telling time (for now), but I enjoy the mix of analog glow and custom electronics — and deciding what to show on the little screen.

EEG at home

I’ve been experimenting with an EMOTIV EPOC X EEG headset: looking at how my own signals change with different tasks (like gaming), watching the frequency bands move in real-time, and exploring how a small personal model could map a few in-game commands to EEG patterns.
